This Is Your Brain on Cannabis. The Good Science Matters, Too.

From cognitive recovery to meaningful medical breakthroughs, peer-reviewed research tells a richer and more hopeful story than a seven-point list of brain effects.

Responding to the October 27 Men’s Health feature on seven ways THC affects the brain.

What happens in the brain during a cannabis high is only part of the story. An October 27 Men’s Health feature explored seven effects of THC, from memory and mood to coordination and pain. But behind those familiar descriptions lies a larger body of science: research on recovery, real-world cognition, emotional experience and medicines that are already helping patients.

Those positive findings deserve to be part of the same conversation. Here is what the studies add.

01 / MEMORY

A brain capable of recovery

Yes, THC can temporarily interrupt short-term recall. But a moment of forgetfulness is not a diagnosis of lasting damage. A 69-study meta-analysis involving 8,727 people found that cognitive differences were no longer statistically significant in the subset of studies requiring more than 72 hours without cannabis. A 2026 review of 26 studies also documented substantial cognitive recovery after cessation.12

Researchers have examined the bigger picture, too: a 2026 study using UK Biobank and U.S. veteran data found no faster cognitive decline associated with cannabis history and no statistically significant association with dementia in its adjusted veteran analysis.3

02 / EMOTIONS

Stress relief has a scientific basis

Relaxation is more than an anecdote. In a randomized, double-blind study, a lower dose of THC reduced the distress participants reported after a laboratory stress test. They also perceived the task as less threatening.4

Another controlled experiment found that CBD reduced public-speaking anxiety among people with social anxiety disorder. These findings give scientific weight to two reasons people have long been interested in cannabinoids: calmer moments and emotional relief.5

03 / COORDINATION

From brain effects to movement relief

A discussion of coordination is incomplete without the people whose muscle stiffness makes movement painful. A major Cochrane review found that a standardized THC/CBD medicine improved patient-reported muscle spasticity in multiple sclerosis. Across five trials and 1,143 people, the treatment was associated with roughly 22 additional patients per 100 reporting meaningful relief compared with placebo.6

The review also found improved patient-reported overall well-being across eight trials. For people living with neurological symptoms, those are significant additions to the conversation.6

04 / DECISION-MAKING

What longer-term brain studies actually find

An intoxication snapshot is not the same thing as a measure of someone's long-term abilities. A 2024 JAMA Network Open study followed adults beginning medical cannabis use and found no significant change after one year in brain activation during working-memory, reward-processing or inhibitory-control tasks.7

In a separate small longitudinal study, 22 medical cannabis patients showed improved speed and accuracy on an executive-function task after three months, alongside self-reported improvements in clinical symptoms. A fair conversation about cognition includes findings like these.8

05 / REWARD

The motivation stereotype meets the evidence

The familiar image of an unmotivated cannabis user does not consistently survive direct testing. In the CannTeen study of 274 adolescents and adults, researchers found no greater apathy or lower willingness to work for rewards among users assessed outside acute intoxication.9

Another study found frequent users were more likely to choose effortful tasks for a reward than nonusers. Human brain scans have also documented normalization of CB1 cannabinoid-receptor availability after about four weeks of monitored abstinence, illustrating the brain's adaptability.1011

06 / CREATIVITY

Joy, music and the creative experience

There is a positive side to altered perception that deserves attention. Two randomized experiments found that cannabis increased joviality, leading participants to evaluate creative ideas more favorably. That is a measurable change in how the creative experience feels, even without a measured improvement in creativity itself.12

Music offers another window. In a 2025 study, 104 cannabis users reported greater musical immersion and perceived auditory sensitivity while high. Their accounts highlight enjoyment and emotional engagement as experiences worth studying rather than dismissing.13

07 / PAIN & MEDICINE

The strongest positive evidence is clinical

A 2026 review of 25 randomized trials involving 2,303 people found modest pain relief with certain THC-containing medicines. And in a 2024 cancer trial, adding an oral THC/CBD extract to standard treatment increased the complete-response rate for chemotherapy-related vomiting from 8% to 24%.1415

Perhaps most strikingly, a landmark New England Journal of Medicine trial found that purified CBD significantly reduced convulsive seizures in patients with Dravet syndrome. A separate review encompassing 39 randomized trials found that medical cannabinoids improved some sleep outcomes for people with chronic pain.1617

If we're going to talk about what cannabis does to the brain, the therapeutic breakthroughs, recovery findings and human experiences deserve equal space.

Beyond the brain, a broader case

The medical evidence has already led to approved therapies. The U.S. Food and Drug Administration has authorized purified CBD for seizures associated with several serious epilepsy conditions, as well as cannabis-related prescription medicines for other specific indications. These are regulated medical uses of particular compounds and formulations.19

There is a wider social benefit to consider, too. A JAMA Network Open study found that recreational legalization was associated with a 76.3% reduction in adult cannabis-possession arrest rates in states that had not previously decriminalized possession.18

It is time for the public conversation to reflect the science in full: a brain capable of recovery, meaningful relief for patients, documented enjoyment and medical treatments supported by randomized trials. Cannabis is not merely a collection of effects during intoxication. It is also a subject of serious, productive scientific discovery.

About this perspective

This is an affirmative, research-based counterperspective to a short Men’s Health article, not a claim that every cannabis product has identical effects. Medical findings refer to the specific THC or CBD preparations and populations studied. Each numbered citation below links to the original scientific publication or an authoritative evidence review.
